# 2004 Honda CR-V (K24A1)

Aaron's older car: 2nd-generation CR-V (RD4–RD9 chassis, 2002–2006), 2.4 L **K24A1** i-VTEC
inline-4. Most US cars have the 5-speed automatic. Diagnosed with the [[obdlink-ex]] through
`carlink`. For how this car relates to the other Honda, see [[odyssey-2016]].

## Recognising it from the VIN

Checked with NHTSA vPIC on 2026-11-07 (`DecodeVinValues`). confidence: verified.

| VIN position | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 (WMI) | `JHL` (Japan-built) **or** `SHS` (Swindon, UK-built) | Honda light truck; many US CR-Vs were UK-built |
| 4–5 | `RD` | CR-V 2nd gen (RD) |
| 6–8 | e.g. `684` LX FWD, `788` EX 4WD | trim/drive/transmission |
| 10 | `4` | model year 2004 |

So `^(JHL|SHS)RD.{4}4` matches a 2004 CR-V. vPIC returns `EngineModel=K24A1` for these VINs.
Note: Mode 09 PID 02 (VIN) may **not** be supported on a 2004 ISO 9141-2 PCM — Mode 09 VIN
reporting was only required from MY2005 onward, so `carlink` should fall back to asking the user.
(confidence: unverified for this car — test with `0902`.)

## Diagnostic link

| Item | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Protocol | **ISO 9141-2** (K-line, pin 7, 10.4 kbaud, 5-baud init). ELM/STN protocol `3` (`ATSP3`) | community — PinoutGuide lists the 2002+ CR-V (148 hp) as ISO 9141-2; Honda used ISO 9141 through ~2006 |
| Answering ECU | One: the **PCM** (A/T) / ECM (M/T). With `ATH1` the K-line reply header is `48 6B 10 …`, source address `10` | community (standard ISO 9141-2 engine address; observe on the car) |
| Separate TCM? | **No.** The PCM runs the automatic. A/T faults surface to a generic tool as **P0700** plus the specific P07xx code from the same PCM | verified — service manual "Description of On-Board Diagnostics" and P0700 chart |
| Other modules (ABS, SRS, body multiplex) | Not reachable with generic OBD; Honda supplies no generic-scan-tool procedure for ABS. They need HDS or blink codes | verified (manual says "Honda does not provide instructions") |
| Honda-enhanced data | None documented for this K-line PCM with a generic adapter. Treat as HDS-only | unverified (absence of evidence) |

If auto-detect is slow or fails, force `ATSP3`. The car must be ignition ON (II). A cold K-line init
takes ~2–3 s; see [[obdlink-ex]] and [[elm327-at-commands]].

## Readiness monitors

From the 2004 Honda service manual "Monitors, Trips, Drive Cycles and Readiness Codes".
confidence: verified.

| Monitor | Type | Drive to complete (generic scan tool) |
|---|---|---|
| Catalyst | non-continuous | ECT ≥ 158 °F, IAT ≥ 20 °F, steady > 25 mph; stop-and-go with short cruises, ~5 mi |
| EVAP | non-continuous | Cold start with ECT and IAT both 32–95 °F and within 12 °F of each other; ~2.5 mi stop-and-go |
| A/F sensor (O2 sensor) | non-continuous | ECT ≥ 140 °F; include a 5 s closed-throttle decel; ~3.5 mi |
| A/F sensor heater | non-continuous | idle 1 minute |
| Misfire, fuel system, comprehensive components | continuous | always "available" |

No EGR or secondary-air monitor is listed. The upstream sensor is a wide-band **A/F sensor**,
the downstream a conventional HO2S. **MIL self-check:** key ON, engine off — the MIL lights
15–20 s; if it then **blinks five times**, one or more readiness codes are incomplete. See
[[readiness-monitors]].

## Clearing codes on this car

A generic Service 04 clear is, in Honda's words, an **ECM/PCM reset** ("Reset the ECM/PCM with
the Scan Tool or the HDS: … use the scan tool or the HDS to clear the DTC"). After any reset Honda
says to run the **idle learn** ([[honda-idle-relearn]]). It also clears all readiness codes. It does
**not** reset the "MAINT REQ'D" light ([[honda-maintenance-minder]]). There is no CKP pattern
learn listed for this PCM. See [[clearing-codes]].

## Known problems

Detail and fixes are on [[honda-common-dtcs]]. Most common, in order of how often they come up:

- **A/C compressor / clutch failure.** This is the top NHTSA complaint theme for MY2004, with
  "compressor" in 95 of 501 complaints. Honda TSB 09-076 covers 2002–04 CR-V "A/C system does
  not work": the compressor fails internally and spreads metal debris ("black death"). If metal or
  grey/black residue is found in the discharge port, the compressor *and* contaminated parts must be
  replaced. A clutch-only failure (worn plate, too large an air gap, open coil) is the cheaper
  case. No DTC is set. verified (TSB) + community.
- **P1457** EVAP leak, canister side. Usually the canister vent shut valve. TSB 08-016 covers
  2002–04 CR-V (EVAP solenoid valve kit), and Service News 2004 says to check for a clogged purge line.
- **P2646 / P2647** VTEC (rocker arm) oil pressure switch. TSB 13-021 covers 2002–09 CR-V: replace
  the switch (P/N 37250-PNE-G01, 16 lb-ft). Check the oil level first.
- **P0420 / P0141**: catalyst efficiency and the secondary O2 heater. Fix P0137/P0138/P0141 first.
- **P1298**: ELD (electrical load detector) in the under-hood fuse box. TSB 05-006 covers 2002–04 CR-V.
- **A/T**: P0700 + P0740 (lock-up), P0730, P0780. A/T failures on 2002–04 CR-Vs are a known pattern.
- **P0011** VTC system malfunction. The manual's first checks are low oil pressure, a clogged VTC
  strainer and the VTC oil control solenoid. The famous "cold-start VTC actuator rattle" TSBs
  (09-010, 16-012) apply to **2007+** CR-Vs, not this generation.
- **Driver power-window master switch melting**: recalls 12V486 and 20V768.

## Maintenance highlights

From the service manual's normal schedule and fluid specs. confidence: verified.

- **Timing: chain** (K24A1), so there is no belt interval.
- **Valve clearance:** inspect every **110,000 mi**, otherwise adjust only if noisy. The K24
  has screw-and-locknut adjusters.
- **Spark plugs:** replace at 110,000 mi.
- **ATF:** Honda **ATF DW-1**. DW-1 replaced Z1 (Service News Nov 2010). Drain-and-fill is 3.1 L
  (3.3 qt) and the total is 7.2 L. The normal schedule lists replacement at 120,000 mi; severe
  service is more often.
- **Coolant:** Honda Type 2. First change at 120,000 mi / 10 yr, then every 60,000 mi / 5 yr.
  The change quantity is 5.4 L (A/T).
- **Oil:** SAE 5W-20, every 10,000 mi or 1 year (normal), driven by the MAINT REQ'D light (see
  [[honda-maintenance-minder]]).
- **Rear differential (4WD, "Dual Pump" system):** replace fluid at 90,000 mi with Honda DPSF.
- **TPMS: none.** No tire monitoring system appears anywhere in the 2004 CR-V service manual. TPMS
  reached the CR-V with the 2007 redesign.
